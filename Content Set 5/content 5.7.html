History

Phonograph

A phonograph records and reproduces sound through a physical recording surface. Early versions used a rotating cylinder, with a needle responding to vibrations. Later recordings also appeared on discs. These machines allowed voices and music to be preserved and heard again after a performance ended.

Key points

  • Sound vibrations moved a needle that marked a groove in the recording surface.
  • A playback needle followed the recorded groove to reproduce the captured sounds.
